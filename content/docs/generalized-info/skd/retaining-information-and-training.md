This was an odd video by SuperKawaiiDesu because I personally thought he rambled a lot. I link it just for having a source, but the video can be boiled down to the following:

1. Learning in fighting games is piecemeal

You go into a game, do stuff, make mistakes, win/lose. Go into training mode, look at the frame data for moves you clearly remember giving you trouble, play around to see what kind of options you have. As you progress, things like training mode will become something you use to experiment occasionally rather than drill constantly

2. Effectiveness of labbing varies from game to game

The overall effectiveness of labbing varies from game to game. Complex games that have a lot of tiny interactions and lengthy combos like Blazblue and Guilty Gear require you to be in training mode and learning what shit does.

More straightforward games that value easy-to-understand interactions that are given complexity and meaning by player behavior like Granblue Fantasy Versus and Street Fighter require less time overall in training mode and more time actually playing to see what those situations are like.

3. Watching pros has rapidly diminishing returns at low and mid level

Watching pro videos/tournament videos has rapidly diminishing returns as one progresses through a game's learning stages and becomes a better fighting game player overall. Early on/when learning new characters, they help out a lot by showing the viewer things they may not be considering.

As time goes on, the viewer has probably seen the interactions a bunch already in their own gameplay and it's more along the lines of "junk food" video viewing.

Once you reach a certain skill level as a player, videos of pros and tournament videos become useful again as you'll be able to see how top players overcome various neutral and disadvantage situations against players above a certain skill level.
