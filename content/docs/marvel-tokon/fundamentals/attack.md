## Inputs

### Normals

Tokon is a L/M/H-style game in the vein of Granblue Versus and Unist 2:

- L = Light
- M = Medium
- H = Heavy

Each character has an additional "U" button for their [U]nique attack. For some characters, the U button is similar to an extra normal, for others it's more akin to a special.

### Specials

Specials in Tokon are referred to as skills. Tokon has four motion inputs for performing specials:

- Quarter circle forward (QCF, numpad notation: 236)
- Quarter circle back (QCB, numpad notation: 214)
- Dragon Punch (DP, numpad notation: 623)
- Down-Down (numpad notation: 22)

For experienced fighting game players, this section may seem a little strange or too newbie friendly, but it serves as a backdrop to the following: likely to the dismay of longtime fighting game players, these inputs are the only special inputs in the game and are homogenized across the cast.

I'll be using numpad notation for these docs.

### Supers

Supers in Tokon are split between **Super Skills** and **Ultimate Skills**. The likely nomenclature that'll evolve will be that **Super Skills** will be referred to as supers and **Ultimate Skills** will be referred to as "ults" or ultimates. I'll use that nomenclature here.

Supers are performed with the input 236 M + H.

Ultimates are performed with the input 632146 M + H. This is a Half Circle Back (HCB) motion ending in forward.

**Tokon Assemble** is this game's big flashy high-damage super. It's performed with the input 236 L + [A]ssist button. Tokon Assemble is a bit different than most supers because it requires three conditions to be fulfilled:

1. The user has full meter (150 skill gauge)
2. A fully assembled team (fully unlocked Assembly Gauge)
3. The user must be in last game, last round where losing the round would lose the user the match

If Tokon Assemble connects and the user is down on Assembly Gauge, it will completely refill the Assembly Gauge.

## Truly basic combos

Advanced/experienced players will likely want to skip this section, but it's useful for you to know if you're onboarding someone new to fighting games to Tokon.

### Link attacks

Every character can input normal moves in succession after a prior normal hits or is blocked. Tokon calls this a **Link Attack**. The in-game guide describes this mechanic poorly.

When performing a link attack, you can only use each normal strength once within the link attack, and the normals have to be used from weakest to strongest.

However, link attacks are special in that they can use *combinations of standing and crouching normals*. An example link attack could be `2L > 5M > 2M > 5H`.

This is a familiar mechanic to tag fighter players (i.e., Dragonball FighterZ, Marvel vs Capcom) and Unist players, who describe this kind of mechanic as a **magic sequence**. It will feel slightly similar to Guilty Gear +R and Xrd players who are used to ArcSys' **gatling** mechanic.

Additionally, you can skip buttons in a link attack: you can start from mediums and go into heavies, or you can start from lights and go directly into heavies.

### Autocombos

Link attacks can be performed with one normal strength. If you opt to perform a link attack with one normal strength, ending the link attack while standing/in neutral stance will trigger a finisher. It gives negligible oki, sometimes unusable, but it resets you and your opponent to neutral-ish spacing.

By pressing a specific button strength on hit a certain number of times, you'll trigger an autocombo. Based on the number of button presses in the autocombo, you'll get anything ranging from a basic ender to a combo ending in a super.

These autocombos will do far less damage than a normal link attack mixed with assists and specials, but they're very easy to do. New players will want to try to graduate from autocombos over time as the damage handicap will impede the ability to enjoy the game.

#### Light autocombo

By pressing L five times on hit, you'll perform a light autocombo: the first three hits are a small link attack, the fourth is a strike with all your **active** teammates striking your opponent, and the fifth is a special to end the autocombo.

If you have the meter and press L a sixth time, you'll trigger a Tokon Assemble.

#### Medium autocombo

By pressing M three times on hit, you'll perform a medium autocombo: the first two hits are a normal into a finisher and the third hit is a special. If you have the meter, a fourth hit will trigger a super.

If you don't have the meter for a super, a fourth hit won't do anything extra.

#### Heavy autocombo

A heavy autocombo follows the same input structure as a medium one, except your fourth hit will trigger an ultimate. If you don't have the meter for an ultimate, nothing will happen on the fourth hit.

#### Mix and match autocombo enders

You can tweak autocombos slightly: once the finisher makes contact, you can do 6H to use the H version of the autocombo's skill, then press 5M to activate a super automatically.

Again, autocombos will scale your damage heavily, but they're handy to players of all skill levels if you just need to land one autocombo into super to end a round.

## Attacking with your team

### The assist button

Pressing [A]ssist + Forward (6A) performs a projectile assist. The projectile will come from your character in the 3 o'clock position.

2A allows your teammate at 6 o'clock to strike your opponent, and pressing T afterward allows you to swap places with them.

4A "performs a powerful unique attack distinct to the character's traits" - the teammate at 9 o'clock will likely end up being your setplay teammate who helps you create oppressive situations on oki or in blockstrings.

All of these assists can be performed as special **aerial assists** from maximum jump height: you must jump twice to have them appear midair. Aerial assists aim down towards the ground.

### Universal overhead

L + A performs "Assemble Smash" which is the game's universal overhead. On hit, you'll deal some damage alongside one of your teammates automatically. 

If you have more than one bar in your Assembly Gauge (the bar directly underneath your health), this will automatically trigger your first teammate to strike your opponent.

Sometimes you'll land these after swapping places with a teammate, who will need some time to recharge. To ensure a successful follow-up, you can hold left, right, or down to activate an available teammate in your recharging teammate's place.

### Super assemble

You can follow up a super with another super from one of your teammates. After landing a super (236MH), you can either press A as the super ends or hold A through the entire super cutscene to have an ally follow up with their own super.

Remember that your teammate's super will also cost additional meter in addition to one bar of the Assembly Gauge.
