Tag fighter pressure has its own special flavor of fighting game pressure, and it will be easier or harder to intuit based on your fighting games of choice and overall experience.

### Pressure & frametrapping

Pressing 5A in neutral summons your shooter.

If you press 5A during the active frames of a move (i.e., special > 5A), your shooter will do a generic assist where they strike your opponent.

Generic assists have fast startup and are the same across the cast. 

Generic assists also have low blockstun, so you don't get high/low mixups, but you do get another sequence starting with your fastest normal.

Since generic assists have low blockstun, you want to call the generic assist at the very end of your special so you have as much advantage as possible.

Examples:

- Captain America's 22L hits twice, so you'd press 5A after the second hit of the shield creates the tallest energy pillar
- Magik's 214L hits twice, so you'd press 5A as Magik's second swing is blocked and goes past her opponent

You don't have to be perfect with the *exact* timing as the last hit ends, but the later you're able to call the assist while keeping the opponent in blockstun, the more advantage you'll have.

Putting together everything we have now, after we do the appropriate framekilling so we have a well-timed meaty, we can do a sequence that looks like:

`5L > 5M > 5H xx special > 5A > 5L > 5M > 5H`

But you're minus & punishable if they block that followup link attack, and you don't have anything else really going for you if you do a safe special ender. 

If you're in the low ranks and confident about returning to neutral vs whoever you're playing against, that's fine, but you're gonna want to keep up the pressure and make something happen.

### The foundational tag fighter mixup

Following along with the video, after you press 5A after that first link attack, the generic assist will hit and you'll do the subsequent link attack, but what you can do *as you're wrapping up the link attack* is cancel the 5H into a safe special and simultaneously **tag in your teammate**.

Your main character will complete their special during the swap, which protects your teammate from getting attacked as they become your new main character.

Brian goes over two mixups in the video: one for characters with overheads that aren't the universal overhead, and another for characters without separate overheads.

### Some notes and caveats

The basic principle shown in Brian's vid is universal. Some character like Magik can't do the whole `link attack > special > assist > link attack > special` loop *exactly*, but get something similar.
